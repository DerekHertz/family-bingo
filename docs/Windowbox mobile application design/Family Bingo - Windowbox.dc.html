Family Bingo · Frontend design system · v1 · Jul 2026

Windowbox

A japandi surface with a living layer. Paper, clay and moss hold the structure; the only thing that moves or brightens is growth. Twenty-five plots on a windowsill — you plant in December, and by August the box has filled in.

01
The board is the product
One screen you open a hundred times a year. Everything else is a visit. 25 tiles, always visible, never scrolled.
02
Quiet until it grows
The interface is paper. Colour and motion are reserved for progress — so a single sprout reads across the room.
03
Nothing scolds
No red, no empty-streak warning, no "behind pace". A dormant tile is dormant, not failing (§7.5, §4.3).
04
An 8-year-old can drive it
Every primary action is one tap on a target ≥ 44pt. Growth is legible without reading a single word.

§3  Motifs

Seven shapes, all of them CSS

Every motif in this system is a rectangle with a border-radius, a conic gradient, or a rotated square. Nothing is traced, licensed, or generated. That is a deliberate IP decision (§9) and it also means the whole visual language survives a theme change, renders at any density, and costs zero kilobytes.

The growth ladder

Five states, derived from COUNT(increments) / target and nothing else (§12.1 — never a stored flag). The tile silhouette changes at every step, so state survives greyscale, colour-blindness, and a glance from across the kitchen.

Dormant
0%
Empty well. Not grey, not sad — just unplanted.
Seeded
1 – 17%
First increment. The seed appears the instant of the tap.
Sprouting
18 – 81%
Stem height tracks progress continuously — not in steps. The leaf rides at its midpoint and grows with it.
Budding
82 – 99%
The head opens at the stem tip. Narrow petals, and the first warm pixels on the board.
Complete
100%
Stem and leaf fall away. Fill + bloom + check + hatch — four redundant cues (§A2).
Past 100% nothing happens
A Member who logs 160 of 150 walks sees the same tile as at 150. Overshoot is celebrated once, in Wrapped ("most exceeded target", §20.4) — never on the board, where it would quietly re-introduce a ladder.
§4  The board — three directions

Twenty-five plots, 402pt of glass

All three use the same palette, type, data and grid maths — 20pt margins, 7pt gutters, (402−40−28)/5 = 66.8pt tiles, comfortably over the 44pt floor (§A3). They differ only in how a tile carries progress. You picked 1a — every screen after this section is drawn in it.

§6  Authoring, sharpening, the centre, swaps

The four screens where somebody types

A board has to be written before it can grow. These four flows all live at the edges of the year — writing the twenty-four, letting the model tidy your wording, settling the middle square, and changing your mind once or twice. Each one obeys the same three rules: never block on a model, never sort people, never delete what somebody already did.

§8  Dark mode

Not an inversion — a night garden

Flipping the neutrals is the easy half. The hard half is the ladder: a 2px moss stem that reads beautifully on cream disappears on charcoal, and a soil tint that's a whisper on paper becomes a black rectangle. So the neutrals invert, the accents lift, and one token changes job entirely.

Default is Match the phone. Neither mode is the "real" one, and no screen offers a per-family override — a board should look the same on the kitchen tablet as it does in bed.

§9  Platforms

One system, two sets of manners

Palette, type, motifs and tile logic are identical. What changes is chrome, navigation and touch minimums — the parts each OS's users read as "correct". Nothing in the board changes: the 5×5 is the app's own object, not a platform widget.

§10  Motion

Four animations, and that's the budget

Motion is the reward. If everything moves, nothing is a reward. Screen transitions are platform-default and unstyled; these four are the only bespoke animations in the app.

120ms
Tap
Tile scales to 0.96 and back. ease-out. Fires on touch-down, not on server response — the increment is optimistic (§17.2).
320ms
Grow
Soil tint rises and the stem extends together. cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1). A new leaf fades in over the last 120ms.
520ms
Complete
Moss wipes upward from the soil line; stem and leaf fade beneath it; the head travels to tile centre, scales 18 → 27pt and its petals widen 26° → 32° as it opens. Check stamps at 380ms with a success haptic. Once per Tile, ever (§12.2).
5 × 60ms
Line
The five tiles pulse in sequence along the line's direction, then a clay hairline draws through them. Diagonals run corner to corner.
Reduce Motion
All four collapse to a 150ms crossfade between start and end state. The haptics stay — they're the part that actually carries the reward, and they're not motion.
§11  Accessibility

A 5×5 grid is a screen-reader problem

A1
Every tile is one focusable element with an explicit label in this order: position, goal, progress, state. "Row 2, column 1. Walk a mile. 96 of 150. In progress." Position first, because a Member navigating by swipe needs to know where they are before they know what it is.
A2
Completion carries four independent cues: fill, silhouette, check, hatch. Test by screenshotting the board and desaturating it — if you can't count the finished tiles, it fails.
A3
66.8pt tiles at 402pt, 70dp at 412dp. The grid never scrolls and never shrinks — at the smallest supported width (iPhone SE, 375pt) tiles are 61.4pt, still clear.
A4
Dynamic Type to XXL on every screen except the board, which caps at L — beyond that the numerals stop fitting. Tile detail carries the full range, so a Member who needs larger text gets it one tap away.
A5
A completed Line announces once, on the tile that closed it: "Bingo. Row 2 complete." — not on all five.

§12  Intellectual property

Why there is nothing to license

You named five moodboards. What survived into this system is the grammar they share — warm neutrals, natural materials, restraint, growth as a metaphor — and none of the authored assets any of them are famous for. That distinction is what makes it safe to ship.

In, and defensible
Warm-neutral palette and natural materials — an aesthetic tradition, not an asset
Leaf, seed, sprout, ring, seal — geometric primitives drawn in CSS, authored here
Shippori Mincho & Zen Kaku Gothic New — SIL OFL 1.1, free for commercial use and embedding
The soil/growth metaphor — an idea, and ideas aren't copyrightable
"Bingo" — a generic game name in the public domain since the 1930s
Out, permanently
Any character, creature, mascot or blob resembling one from an anime or game
Any spirit, forest-god, soot-sprite or totoro-adjacent figure, however abstracted
Any "status window" / RPG-stat-card layout quoting a specific series
Illustration in a recognisable studio's house style, including AI-generated in-the-style-of
Any typeface with a bespoke or restricted licence
Stock illustration under a licence that excludes app-store distribution
If you later commission an illustrator
Brief them on this system's primitives, never on a reference film. Get a work-for-hire assignment of copyright in writing, and a warranty that no generative tool was used on a named artist's or studio's work. The one place illustration would earn its keep is the Wrapped cards — the board should stay CSS forever, because it has to render 25 times on every launch.
